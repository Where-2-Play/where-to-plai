# The Methodology — Where-to-Play / Market Opportunity Navigator

This plugin operationalizes the **Market Opportunity Navigator (MON)** — the "Where-to-Play" framework by **Sharon Tal & Marc Gruber**. This page is a faithful, compact primer on the framework itself, so you understand *what* the skills are guiding you through. It is a pointer into the authors' work, not a replacement for their book.

## The core idea: where-to-play before how-to-play

The Navigator answers the **where-to-play question** — *which target market?* — which must be settled *before* the how-to-play question of *how you win in that market* (the domain of the Business Model Canvas, Customer Development, and MVPs). The book's framing question is "**Are you running in the right direction?**" — speed is wasted if the direction is wrong.

The MON slots in as a "fourth step" alongside the lean startup toolset: those tools find product-market fit quickly *within an already known market* but do not say *where* to start looking. The Navigator provides that missing macro view of the opportunity landscape; lean cycles then supply the micro validation on the market you choose.

Three mottos carry the three steps: **Search Broadly → Assess Deeply → Strategise Smartly.**

## The three worksheets

The framework is a sequence of three worksheets shown on the MON dashboard — a shopping bag (the opportunity set) on the left, a map in the middle, a dartboard on the right. The steps build on one another, but the order is not rigid: a **technology-push** venture starts at Worksheet 1, while a **market-pull** venture with a market already in view can enter at Worksheet 2.

### Worksheet 1 — Market Opportunity Set

*"Which market opportunities exist for us?" — Search Broadly.*

A **market opportunity** is the elementary building block of the framework: a combination of **Application × Customer segment** (or sub-segment), connected by a shared **Need**. Worksheet 1 generates a *varied set* of them in two moves:

- **De-linking** — describe your **core abilities** (know-how, a technological element, a rare resource) in their own right, *detached* from the product or customer you originally had in mind.
- **Re-linking** — connect those de-linked abilities, as a whole, to *new* applications and the customer segments that may need them, generating a broad brainstorm of 20–50 possibilities.

The real goal of this exercise is **cognitive flexibility** — staying alert to a wider landscape instead of being a "one-trick pony." An **initial screening** (Is the need real? Can you fulfil it, and better than others? Any restrictions? Aligned with your values?) then reduces the brainstorm to a comparable **set of ≈3–5** opportunities. All options in the set should sit at the **same level of abstraction** — "continents" (broad domains) versus "countries" (segments) — so they can be compared fairly.

### Worksheet 2 — Attractiveness Map

*"What are the most attractive market opportunities for us?" — Assess Deeply.*

Each opportunity from the set is plotted on a two-axis map:

- **Potential (value creation)** — the Y-axis: "how BIG is this opportunity?", judged for the opportunity *in itself*, independent of your abilities.
- **Challenge (value capture)** — the X-axis: how hard it is for *you* to capture — your abilities and the obstacles in the way.

The authors picture opportunities as **mountains in fog**: Potential is the mountain's height, Challenge its steepness, and the fog is the irreducible uncertainty no amount of homework fully clears.

Each axis is assessed through **six factors** — scored separately, then combined *qualitatively* (never mathematically averaged) into an **Overall Potential** and **Overall Challenge**:

| Potential (value creation) | Challenge (value capture) |
|---|---|
| Compelling reason to buy | Implementation obstacles |
| Market volume | Time to revenues |
| Economic viability | External risk(s) |

Scoring uses a qualitative scale of four main levels (**Low → Mid → High → Super High**) plus the half-steps between them, applied consistently across all options. Because early scores are assumptions, an **assumption map** tracks confidence with a color scheme — **red** (no evidence), **orange** (some), **green** (significant) — so the team can prioritize the assumptions that are both *important* and *low-evidence*.

The Overall Potential and Overall Challenge coordinates place each opportunity in one of **four zones**:

- **Gold Mine** — high potential, low challenge — the ideal case.
- **Moon Shot** — high potential, high challenge — high risk, high return.
- **Quick Win** — low potential, low challenge — a safe stepping stone.
- **Questionable** — low potential, high challenge — usually to avoid.

The midline sits at **Mid–High**: Low to Mid counts as low, High to Super High as high. Only an overall Mid–High lands on the line between two zones — a qualitative reading, not arithmetic.

*(An optional Worksheet 2B, "Where to Impact," extends the map with three impact factors for double-bottom-line ventures.)*

### Worksheet 3 — Agile Focus Strategy

*"What market opportunities should we focus on?" — Strategise Smartly.*

The final worksheet resolves the tension between **focus** (concentrate resources to win) and **flexibility** (stay maneuverable). Rather than betting everything on one market, you choose:

- a **Primary Market Opportunity** — the one you pursue with full force;
- one or more **Backup Options (Plan B)** — related, attractive opportunities to fall back on *if the Primary fails*, which must **not share the Primary's main risks**;
- one or more **Growth Options** — related, attractive opportunities that create *additional* value *if the Primary succeeds*.

The choice hinges on **relatedness** — how far resources, capabilities, and relationships transfer from one opportunity to another, across both **product relatedness** (shared technology, resources, value-chain networks) and **market relatedness** (shared brand, channels, word-of-mouth — "often wrongly neglected, a critical mistake"). Relatedness is judged on a 3-level scale (not / somewhat / tightly related).

The logic underneath is **real options**: a small investment now buys "the right, but not the obligation, to further invest in the future." The result is captured on the **Agile Focus Dartboard**, where each option lands in a ring by investment level:

- **Center — pursue now** (the Primary, possibly a parallel bet);
- **Middle ring — keep open** with ~5–10% of resources;
- **Outer ring — store** (do nothing active, just keep in mind — "you don't pay rent").

## Two guiding principles this plugin preserves

**Human-in-the-loop — a mirror, not an oracle.** The Navigator "provides the cornerstone questions, but it does not provide the answers." It is designed to be "a mirror in your face" that works against your biases, and the worksheets are the medium of **team debate** — "not just your opinion versus someone else's." The decision always stays with the team; the plugin never auto-aggregates scores or picks a market. At the same time the framework warns against "paralysis by analysis": at some point you decide on informed, educated intuition, because the fog never fully lifts. This is a *qualitative methodology, not an algorithm* — the goal is understanding, not a number.

**Longitudinal — "breathing" worksheets.** The Navigator is a "learning companion" that "re-calculates the route" as new information arrives, not a one-time snapshot. Each worksheet is meant to evolve: Worksheet 1 is iterative (add, refine, dismiss, revive), Worksheet 2 is an explicit "**breathing document**" (quick-and-dirty first, then revised as assumptions turn into evidence), and Worksheet 3 is a "dynamic tool — more than a strategy, a mindset." The empirical case for this is stark: **73% of startups change their initial market-entry decision within the first three years** (Gruber/Tal) — adaptation is the norm, so you revisit "whenever new information puts your strategy in doubt."

Exactly this longitudinal, collaborative, human-in-the-loop character is why the plugin moves the Navigator out of single-prompt chatbots and into **file- and project-based agentic work** — worksheets as living documents that breathe and capture the team's learning over time.
