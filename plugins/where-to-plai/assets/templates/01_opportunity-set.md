*This worksheet helps you look before you leap — what can you offer, and for whom? There is no single "right" decomposition here; the value is in the breadth you discover. Appreciate the process, not just the outcome.*

## Upper part — Core abilities (De-linking)

Describe what you can *do*, generically, separated from your current product. Functions, know-how, processes, rare resources, special capabilities — and honest limitations. Owned and planned. Use plain language: avoid general labels like "algorithm" and professional jargon, and notice how the product itself never appears here. Almost any technology can be de-linked and characterized in its own right. ## Lower part — Opportunities (Re-linking)

Each opportunity = one **application** × one **customer segment** (or sub-segment), tied by a **need**. Ask: "What can we do with this? Who may need this?" An application can draw on one, several or all of your abilities. Reach for breadth and distant markets. Both halves must be concrete — a good test is whether you can already say how you'd reach that specific audience. Separate buyer vs. user when they differ.

## Initial Screening

Screen each **market opportunity — one application × one (sub-)segment** — never a whole application or segment at once. Record the reasoning against the four questions:
1. Is there truly a customer need there?
2a. Can we satisfy it with our unique abilities? · 2b. Better than existing solutions?
3. Any severe restrictions that would hinder execution?
4. In line with our core values / moral compass?
