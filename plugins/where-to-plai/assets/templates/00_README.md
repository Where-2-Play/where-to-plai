# Where to Play — your Market Opportunity Navigator

This folder is your **living** Market Opportunity Navigator (MON) by Sharon Tal & Marc Gruber. It is meant to be edited, re-edited, and revisited as you learn — not filled once.

## Files

| File | Worksheet | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `01_opportunity-set.md` | 1 — Market Opportunity Set | your de-linked abilities and the application → segment tree you re-linked; each leaf is one opportunity with its status (on the set / screened out / parked), ≈3–5 on the set |
| `02_attractiveness-map/` | 2 — Attractiveness Map | one file per opportunity, scored on the 6 factors; may also hold `<opportunity>-customer-interviews.md` (synthetic interview notes from customer discovery — directional, not research) |
| `02_map-overview.md` | 2 — Attractiveness Map | all opportunities placed in the 4 zones |
| `03_agile-focus.md` | 3 — Agile Focus Strategy | your Primary, Backup, Growth choices and the dartboard |
| `assumptions.md` | cross-cutting | the open assumptions you are learning your way through |

## How to use it

- The **team decides**; the tool asks the questions and holds the evidence. It never scores or chooses for you. Let it be a mirror in your face, not a mirror of your own opinion — it surfaces your assumptions rather than confirming the answer you already favor.
- Start with `/where-to-plai:opportunity-set`, then `/where-to-plai:attractiveness-map`, then `/where-to-plai:agile-focus`.
- When you have a market question, use `/where-to-plai:market-research`. When you learn something new, use `/where-to-plai:revisit` — and date each update so you can always go back and review how your thinking evolved.
- Mark confidence honestly: 🔴 no evidence · 🟠 some · 🟢 significant. Red/orange items belong in `assumptions.md`.
- **Keep it breathing.** Each worksheet carries a *Breathing log* (last reviewed · cadence · next review) — keep those dates current. Whenever you learn something new, Claude will *offer* `/where-to-plai:revisit`; taking it is always your call.

> Empirically, most startups change their initial market-entry decision within a few years. Treat this navigator as a companion that breathes with your learning. Appreciate the process, not just the outcome — the knowledge you gather along the way is indispensable.
