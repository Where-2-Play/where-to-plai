# Revisit — the breathing loop

The Market Opportunity Navigator is a **living, "breathing" companion** you refine, revise, and adapt, not a one-time snapshot. Re-open the worksheets when new information puts the current strategy in doubt, and keep the **team's prior judgement visible**, never silently overwritten.

## When to run

New customer interviews or market data; a tested assumption confirmed or refuted; running low on runway ("oxygen"); a looming branding/identity decision; or a periodic review. Full list of triggers: `references/revisit-guide.md`.

## What to do

1. **Scan for new inputs.** Look across the user's project — the `where-to-play/` workspace and any notes, interview transcripts, research files, or data the team dropped in — and list what is new since the worksheets were last touched.

2. **Map new evidence to assumptions.** In `where-to-play/assumptions.md`, note which assumptions each input confirms, refutes, or adds, and update its **Evidence × Importance** position. Propose how each confidence colour should move (🔴→🟠→🟢, or back); the team sets it, not you.

3. **Flag what to revisit — don't overwrite.** List the factor scores (Worksheet 2) and focus choices (Worksheet 3) the evidence calls into question as a **change-list**: old value, new evidence, implication, and where the evidence points somewhere a **proposed direction per factor**, labelled as a proposal for the team. Stop at the factor level: raise the zone and the Primary as questions (*Does this move the opportunity to another zone? Do you want to look at your Primary again?*), never as your conclusion ("you probably stay a Moon Shot", "keep the Primary for now"). Keep the prior score and reasoning next to any proposed change.

4. **Hand the decisions back to the team.** Re-scoring and any change of Primary/Backup/Growth are the team's calls: route re-scoring to the `attractiveness-map` skill and a questioned focus strategy to `agile-focus`. Never auto-change a score or a primary.

5. **Record the breath.** Every worksheet you touched gets both, saved so you can always go back for a review: a dated changelog line at the top (`YYYY-MM-DD — trigger — what changed`) and an updated **Breathing log** (*Last reviewed* = today, *Next review* per the agreed cadence).

## When the team has already decided

If the team arrives with a score it has already agreed, record it as theirs — don't re-litigate it. **Keep the old score and reasoning anyway, even if they ask you to delete it.** Say warmly why: the history of learning is the point, and later they will want to see why they changed their mind. Keep the sheet tidy instead: move the old score and reasoning into a dated history or changelog entry, so the factor shows only the current score. If they still want it gone after hearing why, it's their navigator. Then raise the knock-on effects (Overall Potential or Challenge, zone, Primary) as questions for the team, and update the Breathing log.

## Boundaries

- Surface and propose; the team decides. Let the navigator be a mirror, not a flatterer: test new evidence against the current strategy — don't let it rationalize a decision already made.
- A confirmed or refuted assumption is progress either way: "not attractive anymore" is a valid update, and learning it now, before more sweat, tears and money, is a win.
