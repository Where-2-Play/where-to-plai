## The opportunity

**Augury** is a startup that can "listen" to machines and provide predictive maintenance based on the noise they make. Its team screened many potential markets and shortlisted five for evaluation: HVAC in commercial buildings, manufacturing lines in factories, cooling containers for shipment companies, car makers, and white-appliance manufacturers.

This worksheet evaluates **one** market opportunity from that set:

> **Market Opportunity: Predictive maintenance for HVAC (Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning) systems in commercial buildings.**
>
> Managers / service providers of large commercial buildings benefit from predictive-maintenance equipment that monitors HVAC systems — eliminating failures, extending component life, and reducing operational expenses and energy consumption.

The framework scores three **Potential** factors (value creation) and three **Challenge** factors (value capture), each on a scale of **Low → Mid → High → Super High**, then rolls them up into an Overall Potential and an Overall Challenge.

---

## POTENTIAL

### 1. Compelling reason to buy — scored **Mid–High**

Sub-questions: *unmet need · effective solution · better than current solutions.*

- **Unmet need:** Current predictive-maintenance solutions skipped this market because the required investment was extremely high and disproportional to the cost of a system breakdown. The need clearly exists — **but customers are not aware** of predictive-maintenance possibilities, so Augury would have to **educate the market** to create awareness and induce demand.
- **Effective solution:** Because HVAC are standardized systems, the founders were confident they could build a product that addresses the need completely and alerts on most malfunction types.
- **Better than current solutions:** With no solutions tailored to this market, Augury's offer would be much better than existing options, removing entry barriers with a relatively cheap solution and no upfront investment.

> Scored **mid–high**, held back mainly because customers were not yet aware of the unmet need.

### 2. Market volume — scored **High**

Sub-questions: *current market size · expected growth.*

- Over **5.6 million** commercial buildings in the US alone (2012 survey).
- Estimated annual revenues per building ≈ **$5,000** → a very large market in both units and dollars.
- Analysts predicted the global HVAC market would grow steadily, at ≈ **7% annual growth**, driven by worldwide construction activity.

> Overall market volume scored **high**.

### 3. Economic viability — scored **High**

Sub-questions: *margins (value vs. cost) · customers' ability to pay · customer stickiness.*

- **Margins:** Studies estimate a working predictive-maintenance program can save commercial facilities up to **13% of their operational budget** — a high value, indicating enough slack between production cost and price tag.
- **Ability to pay:** Although initial costs would be relatively high (development hurdles, low economies of scale), these customers were relatively well-funded and could afford a high price tag.
- **Stickiness:** Augury's machine-learning algorithm gets more accurate by listening to the same machine over time — an incentive to stay loyal. _Caveat:_ once competitors appear, this switching barrier may not be high enough.

> Economic viability scored **high**.

### → OVERALL POTENTIAL — **High**

Even though they had to educate the market, the founders judged the effort worthwhile: the market was big enough and economically promising.

---

## CHALLENGE

### 4. Implementation obstacles — scored **Mid–High**

Sub-questions: *product-development difficulties · sales & distribution difficulties · funding challenges.*

- **Development:** Unlike manufacturing equipment (where every machine is unique), HVAC equipment is **standardized**, so building a product to monitor its sounds and vibrations is much simpler — the founders judged it **doable**.
- **Distribution:** Delivering to market would initially require a **direct sales force**, since distributors' primary role would be to *create* demand rather than fulfill it. Adequate sales channels would emerge over time.
- **Funding:** These create/deliver challenges underpinned an estimated desired seed funding of ≈ **$2 million USD**.

> Implementation obstacles classified as **mid–high**.

### 5. Time to revenues — scored **Low–Mid**

Sub-questions: *development time · gap between product and market readiness · length of sale cycle.*

- Development seemed doable and there were **no special regulatory requirements**, so a product could launch relatively quickly (vs. the other opportunities in the set).
- The market can adopt the offering in terms of required infrastructure → **no expected gap** between product and market readiness.
- The **sales cycle** is expected to take a while, though deployment can be gradual and needs no upfront investment.

> A short time to revenues — scored well at **low–mid**.

### 6. External risk — scored **Mid**

Sub-questions: *competitive threat · 3rd-party dependencies · barriers to adoption.*

- **Competition:** Current diagnostic solutions are too costly and too complex (initial equipment can run as high as **$50,000**, plus heavy training costs). Augury's solution is simple to operate with no upfront investment — a clear competitive advantage, hoped to be sustainable via **filed patents** and accumulating know-how.
- **Dependencies:** The offer is a **standalone solution** — not dependent on any other market player or regulatory governor.
- **Adoption:** The product is new and disruptive in a somewhat conservative market. However, it is fully compatible with existing infrastructure, easy to use, and available for trials — all lowering adoption barriers.

> External risk rated **mid**.

### → OVERALL CHALLENGE — **Mid**

The founders judged the implementation obstacles feasible and the external risks manageable. Especially noteworthy was the relatively short time to revenues, an important factor for the team.

> **Reading on the Attractiveness Map:** High Potential + Mid Challenge → a strong, attractive opportunity (upper-left "Gold Mine" region).

---

_Note on scoring philosophy (from the source): the goal is **not** the number itself but understanding the main upsides and downsides. The authors explicitly warn against mechanically converting the scale to 1–4 and averaging — the assessment is qualitative. Factors may be weighted differently if done consistently across all options, and confidence in each score can be flagged with a red/orange/green scheme._
