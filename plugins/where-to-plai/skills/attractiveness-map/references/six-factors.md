# The Six Factors — verbatim guiding questions

Quick reference for Worksheet 2. Score each factor **separately**, then combine each dimension **qualitatively** (never an average). Full sub-probes are in `worksheet-2-spec.md`.

Show the team the questions for the factor under discussion **before** evidence or a proposed score — they are what the team steers the discussion by.

---

## Potential (Y-axis — value creation)

### Factor 1 — Compelling reason to buy
*"Will someone really want our offer and be willing to pay for it?"*
A **necessary condition / potential No-Go** — analyse it first.
- Is there a real unmet need? Who exactly has it, how do they solve the problem today, and is your offer a "must have" or just a "nice to have"?
- Can we fully address it and provide a *whole solution*?
- Can we address it (much) better than current alternatives — and just as important, why would customers *not* prefer your product?

> The only way to score this factor is to look through the eyes of your customers — go out of the building.

### Factor 2 — Market volume
*"How big is this market, now and in the near future?"*
- What is the size of the current market? A useful lens: what would your expected annual revenues be if you had 100% market share?
- How much is it expected to grow over time?

> What counts as "big" depends on the venture's industry and goals — agree on it for this venture, then apply it consistently across all opportunities.

### Factor 3 — Economic viability
*"Is it worthwhile from a business perspective?"*
- Do you have sizeable margins?
- Are the customers well-funded enough to pay the price?
- How sticky will customers be?

> Mind the economic *buyer*, not just the user — the person who actually spends the money is often not the one using your product. Talk to them.

## Challenge (X-axis — value capture)

### Factor 4 — Implementation obstacles
*"How difficult will it be for you to create and deliver your offer?"*
- How difficult will it be for you to develop the product?
- How difficult will it be for you to access the market?
- How challenging will it be for you to raise funding for this option?

### Factor 5 — Time to revenues
*"How long until you can generate cash flow through sales?"*
- What is the estimated time for development?
- Will we need to wait until the market is ready for our offer?
- How long is the sale cycle expected to be?

### Factor 6 — External risk(s)
*"What obstacles in your business environment can stand in your way?"*
- How threatened are you by competition?
- How dependent are you on other companies or players?
- How susceptive are you to adoption barriers?

> Note: a *lack* of competition can be equally worrying — it may signal no market.

## Scoring scale

Qualitative: **Low · Low–Mid · Mid · Mid–High · High · High–Super High · Super High** — the four main levels plus a half-step for a mark that sits between two of them. Potential on the Y-axis, Challenge on the X-axis. Score **absolute and relative**; keep the time horizon and any weighting **consistent across all opportunities**.

Unclear evidence is not a reason for "Mid": propose the level the evidence points to and show the uncertainty in the confidence colour. If every opportunity lands in the same band, re-check calibration before calling it a finding.

Remember: this is not an algorithm or hard science, and putting the X on the scale is not the main objective — *understanding the upsides and downsides* of each opportunity is the real deliverable.

## Confidence colours (per factor)

- 🔴 **red** — no evidence (pure assumption)
- 🟠 **orange** — some evidence
- 🟢 **green** — significant evidence

The factor colour is **the team's call** — propose one with a one-line reason and ask it separately from the score. (Evidence colours on individual research claims are input, not the factor colour.) Every red/orange statement is an entry on the **assumption list** → carry it to `assumptions.md`.

## The four zones

| Zone | Potential | Challenge |
|---|---|---|
| **Gold Mine** | high | low |
| **Moon Shot** | high | high |
| **Quick Win** | low | low |
| **Questionable** | low | high |

**Reading the zones:** the map's midline sits at **Mid–High**. Low, Low–Mid and Mid count as *low*; High, High–Super High and Super High count as *high*. So Mid Potential with High Challenge is plainly **Questionable**, not a boundary case. Only an overall score of exactly **Mid–High** puts an opportunity on the line between two zones (both at Mid–High: the centre of the map). Then name both neighbouring zones and let the team discuss what tips it — don't force one. This is a qualitative reading, not arithmetic: the upsides and downsides matter more than the exact cell.
