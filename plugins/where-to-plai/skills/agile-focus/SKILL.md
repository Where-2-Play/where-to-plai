# Worksheet 3 — Agile Focus Strategy

Help the team set focus **without losing agility**: one **Primary** market opportunity pursued with full force, plus **Backup** ("Plan B") and **Growth** options held as **real options** — the right, but not the obligation, to invest later — shown on the **Agile Focus Dartboard**.

**Open with a short orientation** for anyone new to the method — 2–4 plain sentences, e.g. *"This worksheet turns your Attractiveness Map into a focus strategy. You choose one Primary market to pursue now, then pick Backup and Growth options, check how related each is to the Primary, and decide how much to invest in each. The result is a dartboard: what you pursue now, what you keep open, and what goes into storage. I'll lay out the options and trade-offs; you make each call."* Skip or shorten it if the team just heard it (e.g. via the `where-to-play` guide) or clearly knows the method.

## The non-negotiable: every focus decision is the team's

This skill **does not pick the Primary for the team.** "Focusing is about saying no" — that "no" belongs to the humans. Likewise every other call: each candidate's role (Backup / Growth / both), what to keep open, store or pursue in parallel, and — when no option can be Primary yet — what to learn next. For each: **present the options and trade-offs, propose if useful (one-line reason), ask, and wait.** Several proposals can go in one short list for the team to confirm or change. Write into `03_agile-focus.md` only what the team decided — never fill the worksheet yourself and hand it over as the result.

## Step A — Choose the Primary

Start from the Attractiveness Map and its Worksheet 2 evidence. Recognise the map pattern:
1. **One clearly superior** (a Gold Mine) → straightforward.
2. **Diagonal trade-off** (Quick Wins ↔ Moon Shots) → depends on risk appetite; a quick win can be a stepping stone to a moon shot.
3. **All closely located** → back to Worksheet 2: decide on individual factors (e.g. time to revenues) or reshapeability.
4. **No attractive options** (all Questionable) → no Primary yet: go back to Worksheet 1 and/or prioritise learning (*Learning mode*) — ask the team which.

**Rule: the Primary must not sit in the Questionable zone.** Then apply the layers the map doesn't show, in order: **Personal fit** (product/market/founder fit — ask plainly: is this aligned with your passion and values? You have to love it, and wake up every morning with energy for it) → **Fit with stakeholders' interests** → **Resource constraints**. Deciding what *not* to do is the hardest part and can come with some stomach ache — that's normal. Take the time; there is no perfect opportunity.

Before the team commits to this highest-stakes call, offer help in one plain sentence, e.g. *"I can also convene the advisory board — a panel that looks at your map and candidate Primary from several angles — or have the `persona-skeptic` agent stress-test the candidate's risks. Want me to?"* If they decline, continue and don't ask again. They provoke and surface assumptions; **the choice stays the team's**. Once chosen, the Primary is *pursue now* by default and is not repeated among the candidates.

## Step B — Find Backup and Growth candidates

Hold a minimum portfolio: **at least one Backup and one Growth.** Propose candidates with a working role — **Backup** (what next if the Primary fails), **Growth** (what next if it succeeds), or **both** — and ask the team which to assess. Candidates come from the existing set or are new related ones; any new candidate must be **added to the set and scored in Worksheet 2**, colour-flagged as not-yet-assessed. Assess as few or as many as make sense.

## Step C — Assess relatedness (two axes)

Relatedness = how far resources, capabilities and relationships carry over from one opportunity to another; more related → cheaper to keep open, easier to switch into. **Founders routinely check product relatedness and neglect market relatedness — a critical mistake.** Scale per axis, as overlapping circles: no overlap = **not related**, some = **somewhat related**, strong = **tightly related**.

- **Product relatedness** — to what extent do the products share: **technological competences? · required resources? · necessary networks?**
- **Market relatedness** — to what extent do the customers share: **values and benefits (brand/reputation)? · sales channels? · word of mouth?**

## Step D — Balance risk and confirm roles

A good **Backup** is related **but must not share the same major risks / showstoppers / core assumptions** as the Primary, so it survives *even if the Primary fails*. A good **Growth** is as **attractive and as tightly related** as possible. Surface the two trade-offs: attractiveness vs. relatedness, and relatedness vs. risk-balancing. Then propose each candidate's role — Backup, Growth, or both (both only with a reason: attractive, tightly related *and* free of the Primary's major risks) — and let the team confirm or change it.

## Step E — Set the investment level

Per candidate, propose one level with a one-line reason; the team decides:
1. **Keep open** (default, ~5–10% of resources): stay informed, build flexible/modular resources, broad IP, a non-excluding identity/brand. **Every kept-open option carries its confirmed role** — if it has none, ask what it is being kept open for.
2. **Pursue now in parallel** with the Primary (dangerous for early startups): only if the Primary is **highly uncertain** *and* the option is **tightly related on both product and market**.
3. **Place in storage**: do nothing for now, keep it in mind ("you don't pay rent").

Flexibility is not an excuse to never choose: slice resources across too many bets and you cannot really progress.

## Step F — Fill the dartboard

Write `where-to-play/03_agile-focus.md` from the team's decisions: **centre** = Primary (plus any option pursued in parallel); **middle ring** = kept-open options, each tagged Backup / Growth / both — if the team gives a kept-open option no role, even when asking you to just write it down, don't write it untagged: ask what it is kept open for, or record it as "role: to be decided by the team"; **outer ring** = the rest (storage). Colour-flag options not yet assessed in Worksheet 2.

## Learning mode — when no option can be Primary yet

Use it when every opportunity sits in Questionable, or the team doesn't yet trust the Worksheet 2 map. Same gates as above:
1. Say plainly why no Primary can be chosen yet, and ask: go back to Worksheet 1 (new or reshaped opportunities), prioritise learning here, or both.
2. Show per opportunity the open assumptions that could move it (its red/orange factors), each with a cheap next test. Name the evidence behind today's view — desk research (with sources), synthetic personas, or the team's own customer conversations; synthetic-persona output is a directional assumption to validate with real people, not research. If the advisory board hasn't run, offer it once in one sentence.
3. Ask which tests come first, which options stay in active learning, and which go to storage for now. Write the team's order and choices into the learning section of `03_agile-focus.md`, leaving Primary and dartboard *not yet chosen*. A "what if X became Primary" portfolio only on request, labelled as a scenario.

## Mindset

The strategy shapes the venture from day one (resources, culture, a non-excluding name and brand, fundraising). It is a **dynamic tool**, continuously updated — "more than a strategy, it's a mindset." The `revisit` skill re-opens it as evidence accumulates.
