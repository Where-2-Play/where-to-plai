# Worksheet 1 — Market Opportunity Set

Help the team build a **broad, varied set** of market opportunities, then screen it down. A market opportunity = **one application × one customer segment (or sub-segment)**, tied together by a **need**. The goal is *cognitive flexibility* — many viewpoints, not one early bet. The classic trap is falling in love with the first idea, so coach the team to **"look before you leap"**: focusing from day one is a myth, and you never know what's out there unless you look.

**Open with the big picture.** Before the mechanics, give 2–4 plain-language sentences for someone new to the framework — e.g. "Worksheet 1 builds your set of market options. First we describe what your venture can *do*, separately from today's product. Then we look for applications of those abilities and the customer segments that may need each one. Finally we screen each application × segment pair together, and you pick a handful to evaluate in Worksheet 2." Skip or shorten it if the team just heard it (e.g. via the `where-to-play` guide) or clearly knows the method.

## Procedure: de-link, re-link, screen

1. **De-link the abilities (upper part).** Ask "what can we *do*", not "what do we *sell*". Elicit technological functions, unique know-how, processes and rare resources; for an **existing company** (not a startup) also existing strengths such as customer base, channels or R&D budget (spec Rule 1.6) — a startup's pilot customers or pipeline are traction, not abilities, so keep them out of the abilities table. Describe each ability **generically, separated from the current product**, with its functions / properties / limitations: no vague labels like "algorithm", only *what it can do*, never *how*. Allow hypothetical or planned abilities ("what if we also had X?"). A patent or other IP is legal protection, not something the venture can do — don't list it as an ability; note it under the abilities table for Worksheet 2 (an entry barrier or sustainable advantage under External risk(s), or an IP milestone on the road to implementation). Write the abilities into the upper part of `where-to-play/01_opportunity-set.md`.

2. **Re-link (lower part) into an application → segment → sub-segment tree.** Don't walk the abilities one by one: brainstorm **applications** the abilities enable — alone, several or all combined — then under each the **customer segments** that may need it, and **sub-segments** where it helps. Use S&M's discovery prompts — **"What can we do with this? Who may need this?"** — and the Innovator's-DNA probes (**"why, why not, what if"**; **"become an anthropologist"**). **Zoom in / out** to multiply segments; separate **buyer vs. user** when they differ. Reach deliberately for *distant* markets ("out of the box"); a *large* brainstorm (20–50 leaves) is normal, written as the tree in the lower part of the worksheet. Once the first applications are on the table, offer the **`opportunity-discovery` agent** in one plain sentence — e.g. "I can also run a broad search for applications and customers in distant domains — want me to?" It proposes candidates; the team curates. If they decline, don't ask again.

3. **Pick the right abstraction level** ("continents vs. countries"). Compare continents to continents, countries to countries: each opportunity specific enough that one could explain how to reach the segment, not so narrow it fragments.

4. **Screen down (Initial Screening).** Screen each **market opportunity — one application × one (sub-)segment** — never a whole application or segment: a bucket hides segments with different needs. Apply the four questions *with* the team and record the reasoning:
   1. *Is there truly a customer need there?*
   2. *Can we satisfy this need with our unique abilities — and better than existing solutions?* (treat as two checks)
   3. *Are there severe restrictions that would hinder us from executing on this opportunity?*
   4. *Is this opportunity in line with our core values / moral compass?*

   Word each outcome as a **recommendation** ("I'd recommend keeping / screening out / parking it, because …") — no ✅/❌ marks, "Pass/Fail" labels or "Result: n pass" tally, even if the team asks for a verdict. The team decides and sets the status (**on the set / screened out / parked** for later); keep screened-out and parked leaves in the tree so the breadth stays visible. Reassure them that dropping an option is a win, not a loss: a "no-go" shows you searched broadly and creatively, and it is far cheaper to learn now than after sweat, tears and money. Prefer a **heterogeneous** surviving set to maximise learning.

5. **Name and record the set.** Name each survivor clearly and specifically; target **≈3–5** ("a handful"). Write them into `where-to-play/01_opportunity-set.md`. The set is a **living document**: opportunities can be added, refined, dismissed or brought back, but new ones enter only after the screening above.

## Optional: stage a quick debate

Worksheet 1 lives or dies on breadth. When the set feels thin or the team is anchored on one industry, offer a short debate in one plain sentence — e.g. "I can also run a quick debate: one voice pushes for bolder, more distant markets, one stress-tests the screening — want me to?" On a yes, spawn the **`persona-optimist`** agent (bolder, more distant re-linking) and the **`persona-skeptic`** agent (is the need really a must-have, can you truly serve it better?). The **`advisor-tal-gruber`** agent in **"diverge"** mode adds a method-grounded nudge toward breadth (the opportunity landscape). These voices *propose and provoke* — the team still curates and screens. If they decline, don't nag.

## Boundaries

- Propose, prompt and organise, but never lock the set yourself — screening outcomes are recommendations; the team decides what enters and stays.
- There is "no right or wrong" in the de-linking part — encourage divergence before screening.

## Hand-off

When the set is named and screened, route to the **`attractiveness-map`** skill (Worksheet 2) to evaluate each opportunity.
