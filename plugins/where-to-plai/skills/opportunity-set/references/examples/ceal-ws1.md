**Context:** Ceal removes atmospheric CO₂ dissolved in seawater electrochemically, producing valuable minerals as a by-product.

> **Plugin note (added; not part of the authors' example):** (1) The "patented" / "IP portfolio" mentions below describe legal protection, not abilities — in your own Worksheet 1, list only what the venture can *do* and note patents/IP for Worksheet 2 (External risk(s) / implementation milestones). (2) This example shows only the screened set; the brainstorm before it is drawn as an application → segment → sub-segment tree (see the Worksheet 1 template).

---

## Step 1 — De-linking: core abilities / competencies

Each capability is described in its own right (functions, properties, limitations), detached from any envisioned product.

### Electrochemical CO₂ removal
Captures and sequesters atmospheric CO₂ dissolved in seawater using **proprietary nano-structured electrodes** (patented), requires **zero additives**, and enables **~10,000-year** sequestration.

### Efficient, scalable process
Estimated **~$30/tCO₂**; **giga-ton** scalability; **seamless integration** into existing infrastructure; **soft-water** output via calcium extraction; **sensor-driven** optimization under changing conditions.

### Valuable mineral output
Produces **high-quality calcium carbonate** (an essential industrial feedstock) with a **high mineral-to-energy ratio** and low cost.

### Environmental performance & integration know-how
**Carbon-negative** process that reduces marine chemical pollution and abates limestone-mining impacts; designed to **integrate with customer operations** (supported by an emerging IP portfolio).

---

## Step 2 — Re-linking: the Market Opportunity Set

Each opportunity = an **application** (an offering stemming from the core abilities) tied to a **target customer** via a **need**.

### 1. Carbon reduction for seawater-cooled powerplants
Co-locate and build facilities at power plants with very high seawater throughput to generate mineral revenues, save OpEx (via soft water), and deliver clear environmental impact.

### 2. Carbon reduction for industrial seawater users
Serve regasification terminals, refineries, petrochemicals, etc., with **smaller pre-assembled systems** (manufactured with partners), operated by Ceal — owning the produced calcium carbonate.

### 3. Water treatment for closed-loop cooling towers
Provide soft-water treatment for **potable-water** cooling systems; lower economic potential and operationally heavy due to many dispersed installations.

> **Adjacent / backup options noted:** desalination plants, and selling the mined mineral to industrial mineral producers.
