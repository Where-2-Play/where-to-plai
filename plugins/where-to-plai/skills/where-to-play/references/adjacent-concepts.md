# Adjacent Concepts — related to, but not the core of, Where to Play

Source: the EPFL MOOC "From lab to market".

The core of the Market Opportunity Navigator (MON) is the three worksheets — Market Opportunity Set → Attractiveness Map → Agile Focus Strategy — plus their ongoing use over the venture's life. The topics below belong to the surrounding lab-to-market process. Treat them as context and handoffs, not as worksheets to fill.

---

## 1) Value Proposition

**What it is:** The systematic definition and **validation of the value proposition** for each individual market opportunity — who is the customer segment, which **need / job-to-be-done**, which solution and which unique benefit, plus customer interviews (whom to approach, interview structure, biases, dos & don'ts) and the **Value Proposition Canvas** (Osterwalder & Pigneur: Customer Profile ↔ Value Map).

**Relation to the MON core:** **Downstream of Worksheet 1, upstream of Worksheet 2.** Worksheet 1 produces the *set* of opportunities (Application × Customer); before scoring them in Worksheet 2, value-proposition work checks per sticky note "if there is a significant value proposition behind it." It thus supplies **validated input** for scoring — but is not itself a MON worksheet. The Value Proposition Canvas is an **external, third-party tool** (Osterwalder/Pigneur), not part of the Navigator.

---

## 2) Lean Startup & Business Model Canvas

**What it is:** Placing the MON within the larger **Lean Startup toolset** (Steve Blank: "a startup is not just a small version of a large company"), the **Business Model Canvas** (Osterwalder & Pigneur, nine boxes: how an organization **creates, delivers, and captures** value), and the **validation of assumptions through lean experiments**.

**Relation to the MON core:** **Downstream / surrounding.** The Navigator answers "**which** market" (opportunity selection); the Business Model Canvas answers "**how** do I run the chosen market as a business model." The MON is explicitly **one part** of the Lean Startup toolset — synergistic, but conceptually distinct. BMC and lean experiments are **external tools** in the toolbox, not the Navigator itself.

---

## 3) Founder experiences

**What it is:** Practitioner stories — the path from PhD into entrepreneurship and dos & don'ts for early-stage founders.

**Relation to the MON core:** Anecdotal framing, adjacent. Not to be confused with the material on finding markets with Where to Play, case recaps, and the **ongoing use of the tools across the venture lifecycle** — that is core: it carries the Navigator's longitudinal, "breathing" character.

---

## How to handle them

- **Core:** Worksheets 1–3 (Market Opportunity Set, Attractiveness Map, Agile Focus Strategy) and their ongoing use over time.
- **Adjacent:** Value Proposition Canvas, Lean Startup / Business Model Canvas, founder stories. Refer to them as **handoffs** (value-prop validation feeds Worksheet 2; the BMC operationalises the chosen market), but don't rebuild them as part of the Navigator — they are external, established tools. Keep the core value clear.
