# Glossary — Market Opportunity Navigator

Source: the Where-to-Play method by Tal & Gruber (MOOC, book, cheat sheet).

---

## Framework & Steps

- **Market Opportunity Navigator (MON)** — A structured three-step framework by Marc Gruber and Sharon Tal for discovering, evaluating, and strategically focusing market opportunities; answers the Where-to-play question.
- **Where-to-play vs. How-to-play** — *Where-to-play* is the choice of target market (the domain of the MON); *How-to-play* is how you win in that market (Business Model Canvas, etc.). Where-to-play comes first.
- **Lean Startup toolset** — The existing toolkit (Business Model Canvas, Customer Development, Agile Engineering) into which the MON fits as a "**fourth step**" and which it reinforces synergistically.

## Step 1 — Market Opportunity Set

- **Market Opportunity Set** — The selected, comparable set of plausible market opportunities (typically 3–5), the output of Worksheet 1: "a varied set of plausible market options that creates real strategic choice."
- **Market Opportunity** — A combination of **Application × Customer**, connected by the **Need**. The elementary building block of the whole framework.
- **Application** — A concrete use or function you can create with your core abilities (based on one, several, or imagined abilities).
- **Customer / Market segment** — A group of people sharing a common need who respond similarly to the offering.
- **Core abilities / capabilities / competencies** — Used synonymously: know-how, a technological element, a rare resource, etc.; the building blocks from which Applications are formed.
- **De-linking** — Describing the core abilities "in their own right," **detached** from the originally intended application or customer. The first step in WS1.
- **Re-linking** — Connecting the de-linked abilities with **new** Applications and Customers to generate new market opportunities. The second step in WS1.
- **Cognitive flexibility** — "the mental ability to develop a variety of viewpoints"; the real goal of de-/re-linking — staying alert to more of the opportunity landscape, not being a "one-trick pony."
- **Continents vs. Countries** — Metaphor for the **level of abstraction**: a Continent is a broad market domain/vertical, a Country is a market segment. Opportunities in the set must sit at the **same** level to be comparable.
- **Initial Screening** — Reducing the broad brainstorm (20–50 ideas) to a manageable set (~3–5) using guiding questions (Is the need real? Can you fulfill it, and better? Any restrictions? Aligned with your values?).

## Step 2 — Attractiveness Map

- **Attractiveness Map** — A two-axis chart (Y = Potential, X = Challenge) on which each market opportunity is plotted as a point to make attractiveness visible and comparable.
- **Potential (Value Creation)** — Y-axis: "how BIG is this opportunity?" — the opportunity **in itself**, independent of your own abilities. Mountain analogy: the height of the mountain.
- **Challenge (Value Capture)** — X-axis: how hard the opportunity is to **capture** — your own abilities and the obstacles. Mountain analogy: your chance of reaching the summit.
- **The 6 factors** — 3 Potential factors — **Compelling reason to buy**, **Market volume**, **Economic viability** — plus 3 Challenge factors — **Implementation obstacles**, **Time to revenues**, **External risk(s)**. Each is scored separately, then combined qualitatively into one overall score per dimension.
- **Overall Potential / Overall Challenge** — The combined (qualitative, **not** mathematically averaged) score per dimension; gives the Y- and X-coordinate on the map.
- **The 4 zones** — The four quadrants of the map: **Gold Mine** (high/low — the ideal case), **Moon Shot** (high/high — high risk / high return), **Quick Win** (low/low — a safe stepping stone), **Questionable** (low/high — usually to avoid).
- **Scoring scale (Low → Super High)** — A qualitative scale of four main levels (low / mid / high / super high) plus the half-steps between them — seven steps in all — applied consistently across all options. The map's midline sits at Mid–High: Low to Mid is the low side, High to Super High the high side; only an overall Mid–High lands on a zone boundary.
- **Assumption Map / confidence color scheme** — A learning tool: red = no evidence, orange = some, green = significant; prioritize assumptions by Importance × Evidence (focus on important + low evidence).
- **Worksheet 2B — "Where to Impact"** — An optional extension for double-bottom-line ventures: assesses social/environmental impact up front via 3 dedicated factors (Problem severity, Pertinent solution, Impact reach), marked with heart symbols.

## Step 3 — Agile Focus Strategy

- **Agile Focus Strategy** — A strategy that deliberately balances **Focus** (concentrate resources) and **Flexibility** (stay agile) by keeping selected related options open. The output of WS3.
- **Primary Market Opportunity** — The main opportunity "that you will pursue with full force, investing most of your resources and capabilities in conquering it."
- **Backup Option (Plan B)** — A related, attractive opportunity in case the Primary **fails**; it must **not share the same main risks** as the Primary.
- **Growth Option** — A related, attractive opportunity that creates **additional value if** the Primary succeeds; ideally attractive and tightly related.
- **Relatedness** — The degree to which an opportunity's resources, capabilities, and relationships transfer to another; the more related, the easier to keep open and to switch to.
- **Product relatedness** — One axis of Relatedness: shared technological competencies, required resources, and networks (suppliers/partners/value chain).
- **Market relatedness** — One axis of Relatedness: shared values/benefits (brand/reputation), sales channels, and word-of-mouth. Often wrongly neglected — "a critical mistake."
- **Relatedness scale (not / somewhat / tightly related)** — A qualitative 3-level scale per axis (circles do not overlap / overlap somewhat / overlap strongly).
- **Real Options / "keeping options open"** — A small investment now buys "the right, but not the obligation, to further invest in the future." The logic behind Backup/Growth.
- **Agile Focus Dartboard** — A dartboard visualization of the WS3 result: **center = pursue now**, **middle ring = keep open**, **outer = storage**.
- **Investment level: pursue now / keep open / store** — The three resource decisions per option: pursue now (Primary, possibly in parallel); keep open with ~5–10% of resources ("breathing"); or store (do nothing, just keep in mind — "you don't pay rent").

## Cross-cutting Terms

- **Imprinting effect** — The market choice "shapes the DNA of your venture in ways that are sometimes difficult to undo" — the reason the choice is so critical.
- **Breathing / dynamic document** — Worksheets are not snapshots but are revised continuously; this carries the longitudinal character of the framework.
- **Shared language / communication tool** — The worksheets make assumptions explicit and enable team debate instead of "opinion vs. opinion."
- **Assumptions → Facts** — Assessments start as hypotheses and are turned into evidence over the learning journey / lean cycles.
